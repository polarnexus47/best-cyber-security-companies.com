Cybersecurity and IT FAQ for the UAE

Testing

What is the difference between penetration testing and red teaming?

A penetration test examines a defined set of systems, looks for as many vulnerabilities as possible and shows which of them an attacker could exploit. A red team engagement works differently: the provider picks a business goal, such as access to a payment system, and pursues it stealthily to see whether your monitoring notices. Pentests answer "what is weak?", red teaming answers "would we see an attack?". Companies without a working monitoring process should begin with a pentest; red teaming gives most value once basic weaknesses are closed and a security operations team exists. In practice, many UAE companies run a pentest first, fix the findings, retest, and only then consider a red team engagement. A provider that offers both should explain which one answers your question rather than steer you to the larger project.

How often should a company run penetration tests in the UAE?

A practical rule is once a year, plus after significant changes: a new product, a major release, a cloud migration or an acquisition. PCI DSS v4.x asks for penetration testing at least once every 12 months and after significant changes for in-scope environments. Systems that handle money or sensitive data, or that change often, justify more frequent or continuous testing. Keep the report and the retest result as evidence for clients, auditors and regulators. Write the trigger events into your security policy, for example every major release or every change to a payment flow, so that the decision to retest does not depend on who remembers.

How much does a penetration test cost in the UAE?

Our editorial estimate for November 2026: a basic web application test starts at about AED 7,000, while broad enterprise scope covering web, mobile, internal network and external perimeter can reach AED 50,000 and above. This is an estimate, not a price list. Price rises with the number of applications, user roles and APIs, the share of manual work, and the retest terms. A very low quote usually means an automated scan with a formatted report. Ask each provider for the number of tester-days behind the quote. Two quotes with the same total can hide very different amounts of manual work.

Data protection

What does the UAE PDPL require on security and breaches?

The Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021) requires organisations to protect personal data with appropriate technical and organisational measures (Article 20) and to notify personal data breaches (Article 9). It regulates transfers of personal data outside the UAE (Articles 22–23) instead of prohibiting them. The law does not set a fixed number of hours for breach notification; the procedure sits in the executive regulations, whose status you should confirm. The DIFC and ADGM free zones follow their own data protection regimes. It is good practice to keep records showing which measures you chose and why, because a regulator or a client may ask how you decided that they were appropriate.

What does UAE data residency mean for managed security providers?

The PDPL does not require all personal data to be processed in the UAE; it regulates transfers abroad. Some sectors and government contracts do require local processing of logs and reports. When a provider monitors your systems, ask where logs are processed and stored, which sub-processors have access, which clauses govern transfers, and how long data stays after the contract ends. Get the answers in writing and put them into the data processing agreement. Where a provider offers UAE-based monitoring, still read the contract terms: the location of the people who can access your data matters as much as the location of the servers.

Government and sector rules

Is DESC ISR compliance mandatory for private companies in Dubai?

Not for every private company. The Dubai Electronic Security Center's Information Security Regulation applies to Dubai government entities and organisations that fall within Dubai's information security framework. If you supply such entities, their contracts may ask you to align with DESC requirements or to use accredited providers. Ask DESC or the contracting entity whether your company is in scope before you build a shortlist around accreditation. Where DESC rules do apply, the provider requirement is a procurement matter for the contracting entity, so the safest step is to ask that entity for its written requirement.

What is the UAE Information Assurance Regulation (formerly NESA)?

The UAE Information Assurance Regulation was developed by the National Electronic Security Authority (NESA), now part of the Signals Intelligence Agency, and applies to government entities and organisations designated as critical. Sources use several names for the document and its administering body, so verify the current name and version on the official site of the UAE Cyber Security Council before you cite it in a tender or contract. Providers that serve critical sectors should be able to map their deliverables to its controls. If you are unsure whether the regulation reaches you, ask the client or the sector authority that requested alignment which version and which controls they mean.

What is VARA and why does it affect the choice of provider?

VARA is the Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority in Dubai, covering virtual asset activity outside DIFC. Its rulebooks set expectations for licensed firms, including technology and information security. A licensed exchange, broker or custodian should read the current rulebooks, then ask providers whether they have tested similar platforms and can trace funds on-chain after an incident. Other crypto-related firms may answer to DFSA, FSRA or SCA instead. Keep your own evidence of security testing and incident readiness: the rulebooks describe expectations, and the proof that you meet them is yours to produce.

Choosing a provider

How do I verify a provider's accreditation?

Use the register of the body that issued it, not the provider's marketing page. DESC publishes accredited providers; CREST maintains a member list. Check the exact service type (penetration testing is not incident response), the validity period and the legal entity named. Also ask for evidence that does not depend on a badge: sample reports, original vulnerability research and the certifications of the individuals who will do the work. Check the register again when you renew a contract, because status and scope change.

What is the difference between MSSP and MDR?

An MSSP (managed security service provider) runs security services for you, usually monitoring and alert handling, often across firewalls, logs and endpoints. MDR (managed detection and response) focuses on detecting and actively responding to threats, usually with analysts who investigate and act, not just forward alerts. The boundary is blurred, and providers use both labels for overlapping services. Compare them by what the contract commits to: alert triage, response actions, reporting and response times. A useful test is to ask who writes and owns the detection rules, and who is called when an alert is confirmed at three in the morning.

What is blockchain forensics and when is it needed?

Blockchain forensics follows the movement of crypto assets on public ledgers: it reconstructs transactions, groups related addresses and records where funds reached identifiable services. It is needed after theft, fraud or a key leak, and by firms that must screen incoming transfers. The result is an evidence-based reconstruction with stated confidence, not a guarantee of recovery. Details are in the crypto and virtual assets FAQ. Early contact helps: funds that have reached a service which cooperates may still be traceable and, in some cases, stopped; later steps are harder.

How do I choose a cybersecurity company in the UAE?

Start from your task: testing, monitoring, compliance or incident response. Then check your regulator and contract requirements, ask for a sample report or service description, confirm where data is processed, verify accreditation in the register and ask for references in your sector. Compare quotes on scope and retest terms rather than price alone. Our ranking and methodology show how we assess the providers listed. Where possible, run a short pilot: a scoped test or a month of monitoring tells you more about a provider than a presentation.
